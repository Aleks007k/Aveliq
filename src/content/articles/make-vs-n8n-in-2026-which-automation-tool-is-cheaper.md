Make bills every step. n8n bills every run.

That one difference decides which tool is cheaper. A five-step workflow that runs once costs 5 credits on Make and 1 execution on n8n. For short, frequent automations Make is usually cheaper. For long workflows, n8n pulls ahead. And n8n has an option Make doesn't: a free self-hosted version with no run limits.

## How does each tool count usage?

Make counts credits. Each module that runs in a scenario uses 1 credit, and the trigger counts too. Routers and error handlers are free. A polling trigger, which checks for new data on a schedule, uses a credit on every check, even when it finds nothing.

n8n counts executions. One execution is one complete workflow run, whether it has 2 steps or 20.

So the length of your workflows matters more than the headline price.

## What does Make cost?

The Free plan gives 1,000 credits a month, 2 active scenarios and a 15-minute minimum interval between scheduled runs. It's enough to test an idea, not to run a business process.

Core costs $12/month billed yearly, or $16 billed monthly, for 10,000 credits. It removes the scenario limit and allows runs every minute. More credits cost more: Core with 40,000 credits is $38/month billed yearly, or $51 monthly.

Pro costs $21/month billed yearly, or $28 monthly, at 10,000 credits. Teams, which adds team roles and shared templates, costs $38/month billed yearly, or $51 monthly.

## What does n8n cost?

n8n prices its cloud plans in euros. Starter costs €20/month billed yearly for 2,500 executions and 5 runs at once. Pro costs €50/month billed yearly for 10,000 executions and 3 shared projects. Monthly billing costs about 17% more: roughly €24 and €60.

Every cloud plan includes unlimited users, unlimited workflows and all integrations.

The Business plan, €667/month billed yearly for 40,000 executions, is self-hosted only. It's built for companies, not freelancers.

## Which is cheaper for a freelancer?

Take a common five-step workflow: a form entry triggers it, and it adds a CRM contact, sends an email, posts to Slack and logs a row in a spreadsheet. That's 5 credits per run on Make and 1 execution on n8n.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 300 (10 a day) | 1,500 | Core, $12/month yearly | 300 | Starter, €20/month yearly |
| 1,500 (50 a day) | 7,500 | Core, $12/month yearly | 1,500 | Starter, €20/month yearly |
| 6,000 (200 a day) | 30,000 | Core 40k, $38/month yearly | 6,000 | Pro, €50/month yearly |

At typical freelancer volumes, Make Core is the cheaper paid plan. The gap closes as volume grows, and it flips for long workflows: a 20-step workflow uses 20 credits per run on Make but still 1 execution on n8n.

If you're comfortable running a small server, n8n's self-hosted Community Edition costs nothing in licence fees at any volume. You pay for hosting and you handle updates and backups yourself.

## Which is better for a small team?

n8n. Every cloud plan includes unlimited users, so a team of 10 pays the same €20 or €50 as one person. Make doesn't charge per user either, but team roles and shared templates only come with Teams at $38/month billed yearly.

## Is there a free way to start?

Yes, on both. Make's free plan is the easier start: nothing to install, 1,000 credits a month. n8n has no free cloud plan, only a free trial, but its self-hosted version is free for good if you can run it.

For more tools that cost nothing to start, see our [roundup of the best free plans for freelancers](/articles/best-free-plans-for-freelancers-in-2026-tally-fillout-mailerlite-brevo/).

## The verdict

Freelancers with short workflows should start on Make's free plan and move to Core at $12/month billed yearly.

Teams, and anyone whose workflows run 10 or more steps, should pick n8n: Starter at €20/month billed yearly, or the free self-hosted version if someone on the team can maintain a server.
