## What Does Tally's Free Plan Actually Cover?

Quite a lot. Tally's free tier includes unlimited forms and unlimited submissions, plus payments, signatures, file uploads, conditional logic, and integrations — all within fair usage guidelines. The "fair usage" qualifier is worth reading carefully before you commit, since Tally doesn't publish a hard cap against which you can stress-test your volume. What you gain in breadth, you trade for a little ambiguity on ceiling.

The paid tiers start at €20/month (Pro) and climb to €65/month (Business). Pro adds custom domains, custom CSS, drop-off analytics, and version history. Business layers in data retention controls and email verification. The jump from free to Pro is modest if those features matter to you — but the free plan is unusually complete by industry standards.

## Is There a Free Tier on Fillout?

Yes, and it's free forever with unlimited forms and unlimited seats. The constraint is volume: 1,000 responses per month. For a small team running internal forms or low-traffic surveys, that ceiling is probably fine. For anyone managing client-facing funnels or public-facing data collection, it will become a ceiling quickly.

Small print, big impact: Fillout's custom domain feature sits behind the Business plan at $89/month (monthly billing) or $75/month billed annually. Tally includes custom domains at the Pro level (€20/month). If a branded form URL matters to your workflow, that difference alone shapes which tool earns the subscription.

## Which Plan Is Cheapest at Scale?

Fillout's Starter plan runs $19/month (monthly billing) or $15/month billed annually, and raises the response ceiling to 2,000 per month. Pro is $49/month (monthly) or $40/month billed annually for 5,000 responses. The Business plan at $89/month (monthly) or $75/month billed annually unlocks unlimited responses and adds custom domain support, plus form analytics and drop-off tracking. Enterprise pricing is not published.

Tally's Pro at €20/month carries no stated submission cap beyond the free unlimited baseline — meaning the upgrade is primarily about features (analytics, custom CSS, white-label) rather than volume. If you're choosing between tools on a per-dollar-per-submission basis, Tally's model wins for volume-heavy use cases. But Fillout's feature set, including Fillout Workflows, may justify the cost for teams that need those specific outputs. (Fillout Workflows are available on the free plan, so they aren't a reason to upgrade on their own.)

## Where Each Tool Falls Short

Tally's fair usage caveat is the one thing neither the pricing page nor the feature list fully resolves. If your use case involves genuinely high submission volume — think a public contest or a viral form — you'd want to confirm what "fair usage" means in practice before relying on it. That's a real unknown.

Fillout's gap is cost escalation. A team hitting 5,000 responses a month on the Pro plan pays $49/month (monthly) or $40/month billed annually. Move to unlimited responses and the bill jumps to $89/month ($75/month billed annually). For a growing startup, that is a predictable budget line. But it does mean Fillout's pricing follows a conventional SaaS staircase, where Tally mostly doesn't.

## Who Should Use Each Tool

**Tally** suits solo operators, small teams, and anyone who needs a generous feature set without a monthly bill. The free plan covering payments, file uploads, and conditional logic is unusual. Freelancers running client intake forms or researchers collecting open-ended survey data have little reason to look elsewhere at zero cost. The Pro tier makes sense when branding or analytics become a priority.

**Fillout** fits teams that can forecast their monthly response volume and need a clear path to unlimited responses with a fixed number on the invoice. Unlimited seats on all plans is a genuine differentiator for larger teams where per-seat pricing would otherwise inflate the bill. The progression from $19/month to $89/month (monthly billing) maps cleanly to usage growth, which makes budgeting more predictable even if it is less generous.

For freelancers managing payments and client documents alongside forms, it's also worth comparing [Payoneer vs Revolut Business](/articles/payoneer-vs-revolut-business-for-freelancers-2026-which-pays-you-more/) to see where the real cost sits in the full stack.

## The Verdict

For most small teams and individual builders, Tally's free plan is the default starting point. There is no obvious reason to pay elsewhere when Tally offers payments, logic, and unlimited submissions at no cost, assuming your volume stays within fair usage.

Fillout earns the subscription when your team needs a transparent response ceiling, drop-off analytics, or custom domain support without ambiguity. If you're on a team that has crossed 1,000 responses a month and needs analytics from the start, go with Fillout's Business plan at $75/month billed annually rather than waiting to hit the ceiling on a lower tier.
