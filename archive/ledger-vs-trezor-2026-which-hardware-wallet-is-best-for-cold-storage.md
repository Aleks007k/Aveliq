Trezor publishes its entire firmware on GitHub. Ledger does not. That single fact is, for a meaningful slice of self-custody buyers, the whole decision — but most people have more variables to weigh than ideology alone.

Both wallets use EAL6+ certified Secure Element chips to store private keys offline, both support thousands of coins, and both start under $100. The differences show up in transparency, ecosystem depth, coin coverage, and what you get as you spend more. Here is a structured look before the analysis.

## Which wallet supports more coins?

Ledger covers 15,000+ coins and NFTs across all five of its current devices. Trezor supports 8,000+ cryptocurrencies across its Safe 3, Safe 5, and Safe 7. If you hold assets in the long tail — smaller chains, niche tokens — Ledger has a broader list on paper. Whether the coins you actually hold are in that gap is worth checking on each product's supported-assets page before you buy.

## How do the prices stack up?

Entry points are close: Trezor Safe 3 at $79 one-time, Ledger Nano S Plus at $59 one-time. Mid-range splits: Trezor Safe 5 at $169, Ledger Nano X at $149. At the top, the Trezor Safe 7 and Ledger Flex both land at $249, and Ledger's flagship Stax goes to $399. One-time purchase across the board, no subscription required on either side (Ledger does offer an optional Ledger Recover subscription, but it is not mandatory).

## Is Ledger or Trezor more transparent about security?

Here's the part nobody flags: Trezor's firmware and hardware designs are fully open-source and publicly auditable. Ledger's architecture relies on a proprietary Secure Element and a closed-source OS. Ledger argues the Secure Element's certification (EAL5+ or EAL6+ depending on model) and chip-level isolation provide strong guarantees. Trezor argues that "trust us" is not a security model. I'd lean toward Trezor's position on principle — but Ledger's chip certifications are real, and the practical attack surface for a careful user is narrow on either device.

## What does Ledger do better?

Ledger's ecosystem integration is wider. The Ledger Wallet app connects to 50+ service providers for swaps, staking, and on-ramp directly from the interface. The Gen5, Flex, and Stax models add Clear Signing, which shows human-readable transaction details before you confirm — a genuinely useful safeguard against blind-signing exploits. Those three models also include a Ledger Recovery Key (an NFC smart card backup), which is a hardware backup solution rather than a software one. The Stax's 3.7-inch curved E Ink screen and Qi wireless charging are the most premium physical experience in the category.

## What does Trezor do better?

Full open-source transparency is the lead. Beyond that, Trezor supports Multi-share Backup via SLIP39/Shamir on all Safe-series devices, letting you split your seed across multiple shares so no single backup location holds the whole secret. Trezor Suite, the companion app, is free for desktop and mobile and includes WalletConnect for dApp access. The Safe 7 adds Bluetooth, Qi2 wireless charging, and a dual Secure Element setup pairing the new TROPIC01 chip with a standard EAL6+ chip, described as quantum-ready security. On paper, sure, quantum threats to current elliptic-curve keys are not an imminent practical concern, but the architecture is forward-looking.

Custody method matters more as holdings grow — see how that connects to the institutional side of the market in [Bitcoin's Institutional Moment Is Real](/articles/bitcoin-institutional-2026/).

## Verdict: Which one should you buy?

**Buy the Trezor Safe 3 ($79)** if your priority is open-source auditability and you want the most straightforward, no-frills cold storage at the lowest defensible price. It suits developers, security-minded holders, and anyone who wants to verify what their device is running.

**Buy the Ledger Nano X ($149)** if you need Bluetooth for iOS use, hold assets across a very wide range of chains, or want deeper integration with DeFi and staking services through the Ledger Wallet app.

**Buy the Ledger Flex or Trezor Safe 7 (both $249)** if you want a touchscreen, wireless connectivity, and a device built to last several upgrade cycles. The Flex adds Clear Signing; the Safe 7 adds the dual-chip architecture and Shamir backup. Read the fine print and decide which security model you trust more.

The Ledger Stax at $399 is largely a design and screen upgrade over the Flex. Unless the form factor genuinely matters to you, the $150 difference is hard to justify on security grounds alone.
