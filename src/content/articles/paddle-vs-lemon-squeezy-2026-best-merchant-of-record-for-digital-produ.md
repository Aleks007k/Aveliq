Two platforms. Zero monthly fees. One hidden catch.

Both Paddle and Lemon Squeezy advertise a no-subscription model, which sounds like a tie before you've read a word of the fine print. It isn't. The difference lands in how each platform prices per transaction, what that buys you, and — critically — what falls off the table if your products are priced under $10. That last point alone could decide this for a lot of indie creators.

This comparison assumes you're selling a digital product, SaaS subscription, or online course and you want someone else to own the global tax headache. That's what a merchant of record does: it becomes the legal seller, collects and remits sales tax and VAT across jurisdictions, and takes on the liability. Both platforms do this. The comparison is about what else they do, and at what cost.

## Which platform publishes its fees upfront?

Lemon Squeezy does. Its Standard plan charges 5% + 50¢ per transaction with no monthly fee. That rate is public, simple, and easy to model against your expected revenue. Paddle does not publish a rate; it offers per-transaction pricing with no monthly fee, but you contact them to get the actual figure. If I had to bet, Paddle's rate is competitive for mid-to-high ticket products — that 93% CSAT score and the built-in churn prevention tooling suggest a platform investing real money in infrastructure — but opacity is opacity, and you cannot compare it against Lemon Squeezy without a sales call first.

## Is there a free tier or a way to start with no commitment?

Lemon Squeezy explicitly offers a free-to-start model: no trial period, no contract, no card required, and no monthly charges for ecommerce features. Email marketing is included free up to 500 subscribers, with usage-based charges beyond that threshold. Paddle has no published free tier. Neither platform lists a trial.

## What does Paddle actually cover for that fee?

Quite a lot, based on what the platform publishes. Paddle's all-inclusive per-transaction fee covers payments, subscription management (upgrades, downgrades, cancellations, proration), global tax compliance across 100+ jurisdictions, churn prevention via its Paddle Retain tooling, and 24/7 buyer and merchant support. The 93% customer satisfaction score is a real data point, though it comes from Paddle's own marketing materials, so treat it as a directional signal rather than a neutral audit. There are no lock-in periods, and Paddle states your data is yours and can be migrated.

## Who is Lemon Squeezy built for?

Creators who want to ship fast and pay nothing until they sell something. The free-to-start model with a known, flat rate (5% + 50¢) means you can price a product, model your margins, and go live without a sales conversation. Lemon Squeezy handles tax and EU VAT automatically, supports payouts via bank wire and PayPal twice a month, and reaches 200+ countries. The built-in AI fraud protection and email marketing (free up to 500 subscribers) reduce the number of third-party tools a solo creator needs to wire together.

My read: Lemon Squeezy is solving for the bootstrapped creator who needs the simplest possible path from "I built a thing" to "I sold a thing."

Creators comparing this against a course-platform bundle should also see [Podia vs Teachable](/articles/podia-vs-teachable-2026-which-online-course-platform-is-right-for-you/), which folds a merchant-of-record-style fee into a broader toolset.

## Where does each platform fall short?

Paddle flags a real limit in its own data: products priced under $10 get separate, bespoke pricing. For anyone selling low-ticket items, a $5 icon pack, a $7 prompt template, that's a conversation, not a checkout. Lemon Squeezy's 5% + 50¢ rate gets punishing at low price points too (on a $5 product, that's a 15% effective fee), but at least the math is public. Paddle's Enterprise tier exists for high-volume businesses and includes custom migration support, but pricing requires contacting sales, there's no anchor number to work from.

I've seen this pattern before: the platform that hides its rate is almost always more expensive at low volumes and more competitive at high ones. Whether that crossover works in your favor depends on your average order value and monthly transaction count, neither of which I can calculate for you here.

## The verdict: which platform should you choose?

**Pick Lemon Squeezy if** you're an indie creator or early-stage builder launching your first digital product. The free start, public rate, built-in email marketing, and minimal setup friction make it the lower-friction entry point. The 5% + 50¢ fee is easy to factor into pricing, and you won't need a sales call to know what you're paying.

**Pick Paddle if** your products are priced at $10 or above, you're running a subscription business with real churn management needs, or you want a single platform handling global compliance across 100+ tax jurisdictions at scale. The all-inclusive fee structure (once you know the rate) and the Retain tooling for recovering failed payments are genuinely useful for a growing SaaS. The Enterprise tier is worth exploring once your volume justifies a custom negotiation.

The practical step before committing to either: get a quote from Paddle for your specific product price point and expected monthly volume, then run it against Lemon Squeezy's public 5% + 50¢ rate at the same numbers. The crossover point is where the decision actually lives.
