Most "free forever" plans are free until they aren't. The cap hits, the feature gets paywalled, and you're suddenly choosing between paying up or migrating. So the real test of a free plan is how long a working freelancer can stay on it before that moment arrives. Across forms, email marketing, and newsletter tools, the products below vary wildly on that measure — and a few are worth building on before you spend a cent.

## What does each free plan actually let you do?

The honest answer differs sharply by category. Form tools tend to be more generous than email tools at the free tier, because their cost structure is different. Newsletter platforms, by contrast, gate monetization features early, since that's where their own revenue lives.

## [Tally](https://tally.so/pricing): Is the free plan really unlimited?

Yes, within fair use guidelines. [Tally](https://tally.so/pricing) offers unlimited forms and unlimited submissions on its free plan, which already puts it ahead of most competitors on raw volume. The free tier includes conditional logic, file uploads, payment collection, signatures, and integrations — features that competing tools reserve for paid tiers.

The quiet detail is the "fair use" qualifier on submissions. Tally doesn't publish a hard cap, which is freeing for most freelancers but adds a small layer of uncertainty if you're running high-traffic forms. Custom domains and drop-off analytics require the Pro plan at €20/month (Tally shows EUR to European visitors). For a solo consultant building intake forms, lead capture, or client surveys, the free plan likely holds for a long time.

If you're comparing Tally directly against Fillout before committing, [this breakdown covers how the two form builders differ in 2026](/articles/tally-vs-fillout-2026-which-form-builder-is-right-for-you/).

**Best for solo consultants:** Tally is the default pick for freelancers who need capable forms with no monthly ceiling and don't need branded domains on day one.

## [Fillout](https://www.fillout.com/pricing): How does the free tier compare on responses?

Fillout's free plan caps at 1,000 responses per month, which is a real number rather than a vague fair-use threshold. Unlimited forms and unlimited seats are included at no cost, alongside conditional logic, unlimited file uploads, PDF generation, and Fillout Workflows. The seat-unlimited model is notable: most form tools charge per collaborator, so agencies or freelancers working with clients can add users without friction.

The response cap is the limiting factor. Hit 1,001 submissions in a busy month and you're looking at the Starter plan at $19/month billed monthly, or $15/month billed annually. For most individual freelancers, 1,000 responses per month is generous. For anyone running event registrations or high-volume lead gen, it may not be.

**Best for freelancers with collaborators:** Fillout's unlimited seats at no monthly cost make it the sensible choice when clients or colleagues need access to the same workspace.

## [MailerLite](https://www.mailerlite.com/pricing): Is MailerLite's free plan good enough to start an email list?

For a brand-new list, yes. The free plan allows up to 250 subscribers, 2 user seats, and 2,500 monthly emails. It also includes campaigns, automations, a website, a landing page, and signup forms — a surprisingly complete toolkit for someone just starting out. I'd lean toward this over Brevo for freelancers who want automations from day one, since both are free but MailerLite's interface is more focused on email-first workflows.

The 250-subscriber ceiling is where reality lands. Grow beyond that and you're moving to the Comfort plan, starting at $12/month. That's not a long runway, but it's enough to test whether your list is worth building before committing money to it.

If the choice between MailerLite and Brevo is still unresolved after reading this, [a direct comparison of both for small businesses in 2026 is here](/articles/mailerlite-vs-brevo-for-small-businesses-2026-which-email-marketing-to/).

**Best for email-first beginners:** MailerLite suits freelancers who are just starting an email list and want automations included before their first paying subscriber.

## [Brevo](https://www.brevo.com/pricing/): What's the catch with Brevo's free plan?

The catch is the daily send limit: 300 emails per day, with no monthly subscriber cap. That framing is unusual. You can technically have thousands of contacts on the free plan — you just can't email all of them at once, or quickly. For a freelancer sending a monthly newsletter to 500 people, 300 emails per day means the send completes within two days. Workable, but mildly annoying in practice.

What Brevo offers at the free tier is broad: email marketing, marketing automation, SMS, WhatsApp campaigns, a CRM, and an AI content generator for subject lines and copy. Worth watching: the MCP integration for ChatGPT and Claude, which lets you manage campaigns and contacts through AI tools, a feature with no direct equivalent in the other products here.

The Starter plan begins at $9/month for 5,000 emails per month, making it the cheapest paid entry point in this group.

**Best for freelancers who want a CRM alongside email:** Brevo's free plan bundles more channels than any other tool here, making it useful if you're managing client relationships and campaigns in one place.

## [Beehiiv](https://www.beehiiv.com/pricing): Is Beehiiv worth using if you plan to monetize?

That depends on your timeline. Beehiiv's free plan allows up to 2,500 subscribers with unlimited email sends and no credit card required, the most generous subscriber ceiling of any free tier in this comparison. Every plan, including free, includes a website, podcast hosting, community features, and digital product support. The 0% take rate on paid subscriptions and digital products is a strong incentive: Beehiiv keeps nothing beyond the standard Stripe fee of 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction.

Not exactly a bargain when you outgrow free: the Lite plan is $59/month (or $49/month billed yearly), which is a meaningful step up. Beehiiv branding stays on until you hit Pro at $109/month ($95/month billed yearly). For a freelancer building toward a paid newsletter, the free tier buys real runway — 2,500 subscribers is enough to validate whether your audience will pay before you commit to a $59/month plan.

For a direct look at how Beehiiv stacks up against Substack on monetization and ownership, [this 2026 comparison covers the key differences](/articles/beehiiv-vs-substack-2026-which-newsletter-platform-is-right-for-you/).

**Best for newsletter creators building toward paid subscriptions:** Beehiiv's 2,500-subscriber free tier and 0% revenue take rate make it the clearest choice for anyone whose end goal is a monetized newsletter.

## Verdict: Which tool fits which freelancer?

For forms, Tally is the harder tool to beat at free — unlimited submissions (within fair use) plus payment collection and conditional logic, all at no cost. Fillout earns the nod if you're sharing access with a client or collaborator, given its unlimited-seat model.

For email marketing, Brevo's free plan is wider in channel coverage but narrow in daily sends. MailerLite is cleaner for email-focused workflows, though the 250-subscriber cap means the clock starts early. If your goal is a standalone newsletter with eventual paid tiers, start on Beehiiv's free plan and stay there until you hit 2,500 subscribers, at which point you'll have enough signal to decide if $49/month (billed yearly) is justified by your revenue.

One more tool worth checking before you lock in a payment setup: if you're receiving client payments internationally, [this comparison of Payoneer vs. Revolut Business for freelancers in 2026](/articles/payoneer-vs-revolut-business-for-freelancers-2026-which-pays-you-more/) covers which one keeps more money in your pocket.

Start with Tally for forms and Beehiiv for a newsletter. Switch to a paid plan only when you hit the limit, not before.
