## What Does Descript Actually Do?

Descript's core trick is text-based editing: you edit the video by editing its transcript, like a document. Cut a sentence, cut the footage. The Underlord AI layer adds prompt-driven editing, automatic cleanup, and voice cloning. Studio Sound handles audio quality in one click. For creators who spend more time in post than in front of a mic, that workflow is genuinely faster.

The translation and dubbing feature — 30-plus languages — is the kind of thing a solo creator rarely needs until suddenly they do, at which point paying for it separately would hurt. It's also SOC 2 Type II compliant, which matters if you're producing content under an enterprise contract.

## Who Is Descript's Pricing Really Built For?

The Hobbyist plan at $24/month billed annually ($35/month if you pay monthly) gives you 10 media hours and 400 AI credits per month. That's workable for a weekly show with light editing. The Creator plan at $50/month raises the ceiling to 30 media hours and 800 AI credits for up to 3 seats.

The math is simple: if you're doing more than one project a week or splitting work across a small team, Creator is the realistic entry point, not Hobbyist. Business is $65/month per person, capped at 5 seats and 40 media hours. Priced for small production teams, not enterprise departments — for anything larger, you're negotiating a custom deal with no published figure.

## What Does Riverside Actually Do?

Riverside's value proposition sits at capture, not post. The platform records each participant's audio and video locally, which means your guest's bad Wi-Fi doesn't degrade your master file. Up to 4K video on paid plans is the headline spec. Add in multi-track downloads, and you arrive at a session where every voice is a clean, separate file, an audio engineer's starting point, not a compromise.

The AI editing and repurposing layer (Magic Clips, show notes, an AI agent) is clearly an add-on to the core recording product, not the center of gravity. Riverside also includes podcast hosting, analytics, and webinar tooling with lead capture and HubSpot integration on the Webinar plan. That's a broader surface than most podcasters need, which cuts both ways.

Teams that need the same treatment for calls rather than shows should look at a different category — [Fireflies vs tl;dv](/articles/fireflies-vs-tl-dv-2026-which-ai-meeting-notes-tool-is-worth-it/) covers meeting transcription and pricing.

## Is There a Free Tier Worth Using?

Both tools have free plans, and both are genuinely usable for occasional work rather than being purely promotional. Riverside's free tier gives you 2 hours of multi-track recordings, 44.1 kHz audio, and unlimited single-track recording and editing at up to 720p. That's enough to test the recording quality properly before committing.

Descript's free plan gives you 60 minutes of media per month and 100 AI credits, but those 100 credits are one-time, not monthly. Once they're gone, you're on manual editing until you upgrade. The 720p export cap and 5GB storage keep it tightly fenced. For a creator who wants to evaluate Descript's AI features specifically, those limits will bite fast.

## Riverside's Pricing Structure Deserves a Closer Look

No monthly billing option appears in the data for Riverside's paid plans. Pro is billed at $288 annually. Grow is billed at $408 annually. The Webinar plan is also billed at $408 annually. There's no stated per-month rate for any of them, which means you're committing to a year upfront rather than testing month-to-month. That's worth knowing before you assume you can cancel after one episode.

The distinction between Grow and Webinar at the same annual price is mostly about use case: Grow gives you 2 studios and 20 hours of separate track downloads; Webinar gives you 3 studios, 25 hours, and the lead capture and polling tools. For a pure podcaster, Grow is the natural ceiling. Webinar tooling is priced for marketers running events, not a producer cutting weekly audio.

## Verdict: Which Tool Do You Actually Need?

These two products solve different problems in the same creator stack, and the honest recommendation depends on where your friction lives.

**Choose Descript** if editing is your bottleneck. Specifically, if you spend hours trimming filler words, cutting segments, and cleaning audio, the text-based workflow and Studio Sound will compress your post-production time more than any recording quality upgrade could. The $24/month Hobbyist tier (billed annually) is a reasonable starting point for a solo show under 10 hours of media a month. No polish hides the fact that the AI credit caps are real constraints. Plan around them.

**Choose Riverside** if recording quality and reliability are your bottleneck. Remote interviews with important guests, shows where 4K video matters for YouTube, or setups where you can't afford a guest's connection degrading the master recording. Riverside is built for exactly that. The $288/year Pro plan is the practical entry point, but the annual-only billing means you're making a real commitment.

**If you can afford both**, the strongest workflow pairs Riverside for capture and Descript for editing. They're not interchangeable; they're sequential. Many working podcasters treat them that way already.

A specific edge case: if you're building a media brand that also runs webinars and wants one platform for recording, editing, hosting, and event management, Riverside's Webinar plan at $408/year starts to look like consolidation rather than overlap. That's a different calculation than a solo creator's, and it's worth running against what you're already paying for separate tools.
