You built your newsletter on a free plan. Then your list crossed a threshold, a bill appeared, and suddenly the platform you chose six months ago needs defending. Kit and MailerLite are the two names that come up most when creators and small-business owners hit that moment — and they make very different bets on what you actually need.

The free tiers alone tell you which product was designed for whom. One caps you at 250 subscribers; the other lets you run up to 10,000 for nothing. That difference shapes everything that follows.

## Which free plan is actually usable?

Kit's free Newsletter plan covers up to 10,000 subscribers, with unlimited emails, landing pages, forms, tagging, segmentation, and the ability to sell digital products. The catch is real: the free plan allows only 1 automation and 1 email sequence. For a solo creator just getting started, that is a workable toolkit if your sequences stay simple — but it is a firm ceiling once you want to build layered flows.

MailerLite's free tier caps at 250 subscribers and 2,500 monthly emails. It does include access to all three email editors (drag-and-drop, Custom HTML, and Simple), plus a website, a landing page, automations, and signup forms. The feature breadth is genuine, but 250 subscribers is a tight ceiling.

If you are pre-launch or experimenting, MailerLite's free plan gives you enough to learn the platform. But if you want to delay paying for as long as possible while your list grows, Kit's free tier gives you far more runway — 40 times more subscribers, to be exact.

## What does Kit cost when you need more?

Kit's paid tiers start with the Creator plan at $39/month for 1,000 subscribers, or $390/year (saving $78 versus monthly billing). The Pro plan runs $79/month or $790/year. Both plans are priced by list size — your bill rises as your audience does, which is worth mapping against your projected subscriber count before you commit. Check Kit's pricing slider at your actual list size before deciding.

The Creator plan unlocks unlimited visual automations and email sequences, plus free migration from another platform. The Pro plan adds a newsletter referral system via SparkLoop (valued at $99/month on its own), Facebook custom audiences sync, advanced A/B testing, and engagement scoring. There is also a 14-day free trial and a 30-day money-back guarantee on paid plans, with no credit card required to start.

## What does MailerLite cost?

MailerLite's Comfort plan starts at $12/month, covering 3 user seats, 50 automations, 10 forms, 10 sites, and 5 digital products. The Power plan starts at $25/month and adds unlimited monthly emails, unlimited user seats, and unlimited automations, along with 24/7 live chat support. Enterprise pricing is custom and targets lists of 200,000 subscribers or more. As with Kit, MailerLite prices scale with your subscriber count — use the pricing slider on their site to see your actual number.

The catch shows up when you compare the Comfort plan's automation cap (50) against Kit's Creator plan, which offers unlimited automations at $39/month. For a small business running layered sequences, that ceiling matters more than the headline price difference. MailerLite does offer a 30% discount for nonprofits on paid plans, which stands out as a concrete differentiator.

## Kit: Who should choose it?

Kit was built for individual creators: writers, course sellers, podcasters, and anyone whose business *is* the list. The subscriber-based pricing model makes sense for people who send frequently to a single audience and care about deliverability and monetization from day one.

I'd lean toward Kit for anyone planning to run automated sequences, sell digital products, or use referral mechanics to grow. The free tier up to 10,000 subscribers is arguably the most generous in this category, and the Pro plan's SparkLoop integration is a meaningful tool if growth is the priority. The scaling pricing model is the main thing to stress-test before committing: a large list can get expensive quickly.

## MailerLite: Who should choose it?

MailerLite suits small teams and small businesses that need more than one person in the account without paying a premium for it. The Power plan's unlimited user seats starting at $25/month is a real advantage for a small marketing team or agency managing client lists.

The AI writing assistant (HeyLite intelligence) and full MCP and API access on paid plans also point to a product investing in flexibility for technically capable users. The three editor options — drag-and-drop, Custom HTML, and Simple — mean designers and non-designers can both work comfortably in the same account. For nonprofits specifically, the 30% discount makes MailerLite the more cost-effective choice on any paid tier.

## The Verdict

Kit is the better start: its free plan holds a list 40 times larger. Once you pay, MailerLite is usually cheaper for the same list size.

For small teams, businesses that need multiple seats, or organizations that want editor flexibility and AI-assisted writing: MailerLite offers better value, especially on the Power plan at $25/month. The 250-subscriber free tier is a weak starting point, but once you're paying, the feature-to-price ratio is competitive.

One practical note before you decide: both platforms allow you to start without a credit card (Kit explicitly so on its free and trial plans). There is no reason to commit to a paid tier without testing the actual sending workflow, the automation builder, and the support responsiveness on your account — because the plan that looks cheaper on a pricing page can cost more in time if the interface works against you.

Start on Kit's free plan. If you need more than one automation before you can afford Kit Creator, move to MailerLite Comfort.
