A podcast producer on a deadline doesn't care which company has the better press release. She cares whether her 40-minute episode will eat through her monthly quota before she can export a final cut. That question — how far does my plan actually stretch? — is where ElevenLabs and PlayHT diverge most sharply, and it's the one the pricing pages bury in footnotes.

Both platforms offer AI voice generation, voice cloning, and commercial licensing. But they measure usage in different units (credits vs characters), structure their free tiers differently, and charge at price points that only overlap at the $99/month level. Picking the wrong one doesn't just cost money; it can strand a project mid-production when quotas reset.

## Does either platform offer a free tier worth using?

Both do, though with meaningful restrictions. ElevenLabs' free plan gives 10,000 credits per month at $0, with no rollover and no commercial use implied by the tier's positioning. PlayHT's free plan offers 12,500 characters per month, one instant voice clone, and access to all voices and languages — but explicitly bars commercial use and requires PlayHT attribution on any output.

In practice, 12,500 characters is roughly 1,500-2,000 spoken words, enough to test the product but not to ship anything. ElevenLabs' credits cover Text to Speech, Speech to Text, Music, Sound Effects, Voice Changer, Voice Isolator, and Dubbing — so 10,000 credits spread across multiple tools disappears faster than the raw number suggests. Neither free tier is a working solution for professional output; both are long trials with a hard ceiling.

## Which plan is cheapest for light, regular use?

ElevenLabs wins here, clearly. Its Starter plan runs $6/month (or $5/month on annual billing) for 30,000 credits. PlayHT's entry paid tier, Creator, starts at $39/month, with annual billing available at a discount though no specific annual rate is published. That's a wide gap for a solo creator or small operation. The Creator plan on PlayHT does include commercial rights and 10 instant voice clones, which ElevenLabs' Starter doesn't explicitly detail at that level, but the price difference is real regardless of feature mix.

Here's what the pricing page won't say: ElevenLabs' annual plan is framed as two months free — you pay for 10 months — which makes the math on longer commitments more favorable than the monthly rate implies. A Creator plan at $18.33/month (annual) also comes with a $11 first-month promotion, which is the kind of detail that gets forgotten after signup but matters for cash-flow planning.

## How do the mid-tier plans compare at $99/month?

This is the only price point where both platforms show up with the same number. ElevenLabs' Pro plan at $99/month (or $82.50/month annually) delivers 600,000 credits. PlayHT's Unlimited plan at $99/month claims unlimited characters but caps usage at 2.5 million characters per month and 30 million per year under a fair-use policy.

That number matters because "unlimited" is doing a lot of marketing work that the fine print quietly undoes. 30 million characters per year is a real and high ceiling for most individual users, but calling it unlimited and burying the cap in a footnote is a framing choice, not a neutral description. ElevenLabs, by contrast, states its limits explicitly: 600,000 credits on Pro, 1,800,000 credits (across 3 seats) on Scale at $299/month.

## ElevenLabs: What it does well and who it suits

ElevenLabs suits teams and power users who generate across multiple media types. Because credits are shared across every product, Text to Speech, Music, Sound Effects, Dubbing, Voice Isolator, and more, the platform works well when a single team member switches between projects rather than running one workload at volume.

Podcasters weighing the recording side of this stack alongside the voice generation side should also see [Descript vs Riverside](/articles/descript-vs-riverside-which-tool-should-podcasters-and-video-creators/), which covers capture and editing costs.

The rollover policy is worth understanding before you commit. Unused credits roll over for up to two months, capping at twice your monthly quota, so your total balance can reach at most three times the monthly figure. Pay-as-you-go top-up credits sit outside that cap. It's a sensible system, but it punishes inconsistent usage patterns. If your project work is seasonal, you lose what doesn't roll within two months.

The Scale plan at $299/month (or $249.17/month annually) adds 3 seats, which makes the per-user cost reasonable for a small production team. Downgrading or canceling forfeits unused credits at the end of the billing cycle, so timing a plan change matters more than it might seem.

## PlayHT: What it does well and who it suits

PlayHT's strength is volume output and voice cloning accessibility. The Unlimited plan's High Fidelity clone is reserved for that tier and above, while even the Creator plan at $39/month includes 10 instant voice clones and commercial rights. For a content business running localized audio across 140+ languages and accents, that depth of cloning at a mid-range price is harder to replicate with ElevenLabs at an equivalent spend.

The 20% discount for students, educators, and non-profits is a concrete pricing lever that ElevenLabs doesn't publish an equivalent for in the source data. For a nonprofit audio project or an academic research team, that discount on the Creator or Unlimited plan shifts the math. Enterprise pricing is custom and requires contacting sales, PlayHT publishes no floor rate for that tier.

(The attribution requirement on the free plan is a dealbreaker for client work. Any professional who needs clean output without platform branding should budget for at least the Creator plan.)

## Which platform scales better for a growing business?

ElevenLabs has clearer per-seat pricing and a more explicit credit ledger, which makes it easier to forecast costs as a team grows. PlayHT's Enterprise tier offers SSO, ISO/SOC2 compliance, and a dedicated account manager, useful features for organizations with security and procurement requirements, but without a published price, it's impossible to model against budget before a sales call.

For a startup scaling from one to five people, ElevenLabs' Scale plan at $299/month for 3 seats gives a concrete number to work with. For a larger organization that needs compliance documentation and managed onboarding, PlayHT's Enterprise lane is the more natural path, assuming the custom pricing lands at a tolerable point.

## Verdict: Which one should you choose?

If your budget is under $40/month, ElevenLabs is the only real option. PlayHT's paid plans start at $39/month and the free tier bars commercial use. For solo creators doing modest output, ElevenLabs' Starter at $6/month or Creator at $22/month is adequate and affordable.

If you need high-volume character output, multiple voice clones, and commercial rights at the $99/month tier, the choice is closer. PlayHT's fair-use cap is high enough for most single users; ElevenLabs' explicit credit system is easier to manage across a team.

For organizations with compliance requirements, SSO, SOC2, procurement processes, PlayHT's Enterprise tier is the obvious conversation to have, understanding that you'll only learn the price after a sales demo.

The one thing neither pricing page answers cleanly: what happens to your cloned voices if you downgrade or cancel? That's the question worth putting to both support teams before signing anything long-term.
