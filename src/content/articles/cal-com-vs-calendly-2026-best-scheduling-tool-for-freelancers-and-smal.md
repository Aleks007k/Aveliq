Calendly's free plan allows exactly one event type. Cal.com's free plan allows unlimited. That single gap is the sharpest way to frame this comparison, because everything else — polish, AI features, enterprise pricing — flows from a fundamentally different philosophy about what "free" should mean.

For freelancers and small teams in 2026, the choice between these two tools is less about features and more about where you expect to grow into friction. Both work. The harder question is which wall you hit first, and how much it costs to break through.

## Which Plan Is Cheapest?

Cal.com's free tier costs nothing and imposes no limit on event types or calendar connections. Calendly's free tier is also $0/month, but restricts users to one event type and one calendar connection. For any freelancer running more than a single type of booking — a discovery call, a paid consultation, a follow-up slot — Calendly's free plan runs out immediately.

Once you leave free, Cal.com's Teams plan is $12/user/month billed annually or $15/user/month billed monthly. Calendly's Teams plan is $16/seat/month billed annually or $20/seat/month billed monthly. Cal.com is cheaper on both billing options: $4 less per seat per month on annual, $5 less on monthly.

If you're comparing Calendly's Standard plan ($10/seat/month annual, $12/seat/month monthly) against Cal.com Teams, Calendly wins at the single-seat tier — but Standard doesn't include round-robin scheduling, which most small teams need. Once you account for that, Cal.com pulls ahead on price. ## A Genuinely Generous Free Tier

Cal.com's free tier is the more generous of the two. It covers one user and includes unlimited event types, unlimited calendar connections, email and SMS notifications, Stripe and PayPal payment acceptance, and two-way Salesforce and HubSpot sync. That last item is notable: CRM sync at zero dollars is usually a paid feature on competing tools.

Calendly's free tier gets you a customizable booking page, one-on-one scheduling, a browser extension, and not much else. It's a functional demo, but it's not a working setup for anyone with more than one service to sell.

## What Cal.com Does Well

Strip out the marketing and Cal.com is essentially a well-funded open-source project that decided to compete on breadth at the free tier. The move works for solo freelancers who want payment collection, CRM sync, and routing forms without paying a cent. It also works for small teams that need compliance: the platform lists SOC 2, HIPAA, and ISO 27001 certifications, plus SAML SSO and SCIM on paid plans.

Cal.com is open source and can be self-hosted, which Calendly cannot. For teams with strict data-residency requirements or a preference for running their own infrastructure, that distinction alone may settle the comparison.

The Teams plan at $12/user/month (billed annually) or $15/user/month (billed monthly) adds shared availability and round-robin scheduling. Organizations, at $28/user/month annually or $37/user/month monthly, adds unlimited sub-teams.

The tradeoff is blunt: Cal.com's interface is less polished than Calendly's, and some of the newer AI features Calendly has shipped are absent here. If your team skews technical, that's fine. If your clients or colleagues are less patient with rough edges, it's worth weighing.

## What Calendly Does Well

Calendly's product is measurably smoother for non-technical users. The onboarding is fast, the booking page is clean, and the browser extension reduces the friction of sharing availability mid-email. For a small team where one person handles sales and scheduling, that speed matters.

The Teams plan at $16/seat/month (annual) or $20/seat/month (monthly) adds round-robin scheduling and lead routing. Standard Plus and above includes Callie, an AI assistant that handles scheduling over email, and an automated meeting Notetaker that drafts follow-up emails. These are real workflow features, not just demos. Salesforce, Marketo, and Pardot integrations ship at the paid tiers, though Salesforce sync on Cal.com's free tier gives that platform a meaningful edge for CRM-dependent freelancers.

SAML SSO is available as an add-on on Calendly's Teams plan and included on Enterprise. Enterprise starts at $15,000/year for a minimum of 50 seats, which takes it out of scope for most small teams. ## Who Should Use Calendly?

Calendly suits teams that prioritize ease of use and want AI-assisted scheduling workflows. If you're a two- to five-person sales or client-services team, the $16/seat/month Teams plan (annual) gives you round-robin scheduling, lead routing, and the Notetaker feature, all without configuring anything complex.

The free plan is probably best treated as a trial rather than a working setup. One event type is rarely enough.

## Who Should Use Cal.com?

Cal.com is the cleaner choice for independent freelancers who need payment collection and CRM sync from day one without paying for them. A consultant who runs three types of paid sessions — intro call, project scoping, ongoing retainer — gets all three event types on the free plan, plus Stripe integration and HubSpot sync. Calendly would require at least the Standard tier to replicate that.

For small teams with compliance requirements, in healthcare, legal, or financial services, Cal.com's HIPAA and ISO 27001 coverage at the organizational tier makes it worth a serious look, even at $28/user/month annually. And for teams that want to run the tool on their own servers entirely, the self-hosting option is a category of one here. If your team also relies on forms for intake or routing, see how [Tally and Fillout compare](/articles/tally-vs-fillout-2026-which-form-builder-is-right-for-you/) before building a workflow around either scheduling tool.

## The Verdict

For a solo freelancer: Cal.com's free plan is the practical starting point. The gap between what it offers and what Calendly's free tier offers is large enough that there's no obvious reason to start with Calendly unless you already have a strong preference for its interface.

For a small team of two to ten people: this is closer. Calendly's Teams plan at $16/seat/month (annual) is well-suited for teams that lean on AI features and don't need CRM sync at the free level. Cal.com's Teams plan at $12/user/month (annual) costs less per seat and includes CRM sync, so the comparison shifts depending on how much those workflow features matter to your team.

For compliance-sensitive teams: Cal.com's certifications and self-hosting option give it an edge that Calendly's pricing page doesn't obviously match below the Enterprise tier.

Solo freelancers should start on Cal.com's free plan. Teams that value a polished interface over price should pick Calendly; everyone else saves money with Cal.com.
